# Characters

## Protagonist (name TBD)

**Origin:** Born into a neutral, non-magic faction (see Worldbuilding: Neutral, Non-Magic Factions). Despite neutral parentage, he carries a genuine aptitude in both Life and Death magic — the one combination the Four-Faction system has no framework for (see Worldbuilding: Dual Aptitude: No Precedent). What makes that combination taboo isn't abstract: Life+Death soul-binding is literal resurrection, binding a soul into a living body rather than into an object or the void (see Worldbuilding: Cross-Faction Magic) — which is exactly what he'd be capable of, and exactly why the discovery had to be buried rather than surfaced.

**Inciting circumstance:** A Void party member is the first to recognize what his dual aptitude actually is. Since there's no safe or established way to reveal a confirmed Life/Death dual aptitude, the two of them decide to bury the discovery rather than surface it, and go undercover together instead.

**The double life:** To learn cross-faction magic without ever exposing his true nature, he embeds in two of Craft's nomadic sub-factions as two separate people, each shown only one half of what he is:
- Within Craft's **land** sub-faction, he's known and trained as a Life-affinity mage, alongside his best-friend partner.
- Within Craft's **sea** sub-faction, he's known and trained as a Death-affinity mage, specializing in willing-soul work — binding souls that consent to it (see Worldbuilding: Death Magic: Soul-Stewardship) — and performing it through song and dance, Deathsong-style, which makes his sea persona the natural showpiece for the sea school's side of the competition. His casting partner there is also his love interest.

Neither Craft community (land or sea) has reason to suspect the other's recruit even exists, let alone that they're the same person. Over time each persona builds its own reputation and its own infamy, entirely independent of the other — two separate legends, one person, and no one outside his Void companion knows it.

Because no magic can be cast solo, not even within a single faction (see Worldbuilding: Partnered Casting), his dual aptitude can never be combined single-handedly — he always needs an active partner, faction-mate or not. This closes off the one shortcut that would otherwise let him quietly wield the taboo Life+Death combination alone: to ever cast it, he'd need his Life persona's partner (the land-side best friend) and his Death persona's partner (the sea-side love interest) working together, which would blow both covers at once.

**The competition pressure:** Both the land and sea sub-factions' schools are building toward the cross-faction magic competition (see Worldbuilding: Magic Schools & the Cross-Faction Competition), and each persona has become the performer its own school is counting on to carry it. He obviously can't be in two places at once, and the closer the competition gets, the harder that is to paper over — especially since he needs genuine recovery time after combat, which eats into the windows he'd otherwise use to keep both identities' commitments covered.

**Relationships**
- **Void mentor/companion (name TBD):** The party's Void member who first identified his dual aptitude and chose concealment over disclosure; the only other person who knows both personas are the same man. Also wields magic that alters the protagonist's appearance, giving his two personas physically distinct faces — without it, the ruse couldn't survive anyone looking closely. The disguise isn't a set-and-forget effect: it destabilizes under combat stress, flickering or requiring active concentration to hold whenever the mentor is hit, distracted, or forced to fight — a standing risk of a near-reveal of one persona's true face in the middle of a battle. Plays as a quiet, rogue-type operator — works Void's secrets-and-favors economy through angles and information rather than force.
- **Death-faction companion (name TBD):** A Death-faction contact of the Void mentor's, riding with the party as a favor owed within Void's secrets-and-favors economy (see Worldbuilding: The Four Factions). Plays as a loud brawler, the deliberate foil to the Void mentor's quiet-rogue style, and a new bond for the protagonist to build outside his two personas. Also the party's first real window into Death's "vibrant and loud" underground culture (see Worldbuilding: Faction Cultures).
- **Land sub-faction partner / best friend (name TBD):** His required casting partner within the land sub-faction, where he's known as the Life-affinity persona (see Worldbuilding: Partnered Casting — no magic, not even same-faction magic, can be cast solo). A brotherly best-friend figure; doesn't know his true dual nature at first.
- **Sea sub-faction partner / love interest (name TBD):** His required casting partner within the sea sub-faction, where he's known as the Death-affinity persona. Unaware of his true dual nature at first, but develops into the love interest — and turns out to be carrying a mirror-image secret of her own: a genuine dual aptitude in **Craft and Void**, the other opposed pair on the faction diamond (see Worldbuilding: Dual Aptitude: No Precedent), which she's been hiding just as he hides his. That aptitude is specifically for Fold-forging (see Worldbuilding: Cross-Faction Magic, Craft + Void) — one of the system's true taboos, forbidden because a botched fold collapses into a country-leveling black hole, which is exactly why she can't afford to let it show. Between the two of them, their partnership spans all four factions' magic — see Worldbuilding: Partnered Casting, "A Pairing That Spans All Four." As casting partners, the two of them also share the telepathic bond every pair gets (see Worldbuilding: Partnered Casting: The Mental Link) — which doubles as their one safe channel for an intimacy neither of them can risk showing openly. Open question: how/when each learns the other's secret — see notes.md.

With the Death-faction companion added, the working group is now five: the protagonist, the Void mentor, the Death companion, and his two Craft partners — a full multi-pair party rather than a single duo (see Worldbuilding: Partnered Casting — Group Combat).
