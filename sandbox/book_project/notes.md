# Notes

## Open Questions

- **A hidden fifth class, beyond the Four Factions** — the world may harbor a fifth magical class that isn't common knowledge. What it actually is (its nature, its relationship to Craft/Void/Life/Death, who belongs to it) is still undecided — needs to be worked out before it can appear on the page.
- **Neutral, non-magic factions** — beyond the four magical factions, the world (and its oceans) is also populated by ordinary, non-magic peoples/factions. Existence noted, but who they are, where they live, and how they relate to the four factions is still unworked — to be fleshed out later. Humans are now established as the baseline non-magic majority (see worldbuilding.md — Peoples & Soul Ancestry); still open are specific neutral factions and how they relate to the four. (The protagonist's neutral-faction origin now gives this thread a concrete anchor — see characters.md.)
- **Who sees the real him** — undecided whether the land sub-faction partner (the brotherly best friend, tied to his Life persona) or the sea sub-faction partner (the love interest, tied to his Death persona) is the one who eventually sees through to the protagonist's true self, and which one only ever knows the disguise. (See characters.md — Relationships.)
- **Mutual-secret reveal** — the love interest's hidden Craft+Void dual aptitude (see characters.md, worldbuilding.md — Dual Aptitude: No Precedent) means both leads are independently concealing a forbidden dual affinity from each other. Still unworked: how/when each finds out about the other, whether it's a mutual reveal or staggered, and how discovering their combined partnership can span all four factions' magic (see worldbuilding.md — Partnered Casting, "A Pairing That Spans All Four") lands dramatically — likely entangled with the "Who sees the real him" question above.
- **Danger of self-binding an unwilling soul** — self-binding a soul directly to a Death mage's own body is established as very dangerous when the soul is unwilling (see worldbuilding.md — Death Magic: Soul-Stewardship), but what actually goes wrong is still undecided — physical harm to the mage, the soul fighting for control, backlash on release, or something else.
- **Bloodline origin vs. the protagonist's neutral parentage** — opposed-pair dual aptitude is now established as usually hereditary, tracing back to a bloodline that once merged taboo magic (see worldbuilding.md — Dual Aptitude: Opposed Pairs). The protagonist, though, is established as born to neutral, non-magic parents with no known bloodline tie to a taboo-magic incident (see characters.md — Origin). Bloodline is now soul ancestry (see worldbuilding.md — Peoples & Soul Ancestry), so his soul must have inherited the affinity from somewhere, which sharpens the question. Worth deciding whether that's a genuine, plot-relevant exception to the rule, a hidden bloodline connection waiting to be uncovered, or whether the love interest's Craft+Void aptitude carries the "expected" bloodline explanation while his stays the deliberate mystery.

- **Regulation details and the protagonist's cover.** Life magic is licensed and Death magic is permissive (see worldbuilding.md — Faction Law: Regulation of Magic). Still open: who issues Life licenses and how, what enforcement looks like, and whether Craft and Void have their own regulatory stance. Also open: how the protagonist's land-side Life persona is trained and cast within a Craft sub-faction without running afoul of Life's licensing, and whether a missing license is a further exposure risk for him.
- **Who practices unwilling binding.** The ban on unwilling-soul binding is officially in force but ignored in practice. Undecided: who does it (a fringe, the unwilling-soul wing broadly, or with quiet institutional tolerance), whether anyone enforces the ban, and how visible it is to the protagonist's willing-soul Death persona.

- **Races of origin for Craft and Death.** Life's origin is wood elves and Void's is sky/void elves, but the races that originated Craft magic and Death magic are both still undecided (see worldbuilding.md — Faction Origins & Marriage Customs). Craft's origin should probably be a people known for skill or making, and Death's a people with a strong tradition around the dead, but nothing is chosen. Their wide spread is already explained by their marriage customs (skill-only pairing for Craft, love for Death), so the origin race doesn't need to be a big one.
- **Which races Void has admitted.** Void marries by admission: any race it has let in becomes a suitable marriage partner for all of Void (see worldbuilding.md — Faction Origins & Marriage Customs). Undecided: which races have been admitted, how and why, and how this squares with the existing line that Void only rarely initiates outsiders from Life or Death (see worldbuilding.md — Faction Cultures). One option is that admission is granted to whole peoples, while individual initiation stays rare.

## Story Hooks

- **Soul requests as character/plot fodder.** Willing souls that linger do so to pursue a specific goal, communicated telepathically to their Death mage (see worldbuilding.md — Death Magic: Soul-Stewardship). Each such soul is effectively a ready-made minor character with a built-in motivation — a source to draw on for side plots, favors, or complications involving the protagonist's Death persona or other Death-mage characters.
- **Partner-swap chain as a combat set-piece.** Rather than treating multi-pair repositioning as background mechanic, build an entire combat sequence around the macro layer: three or four pairs re-forming on the fly as the fight moves, with the prose tracking who's paired with whom the way a heist story tracks who's where — the swapping is the choreography itself, not a break in it (see worldbuilding.md — Partnered Casting: Group Combat).

## Plot Pacing

- **Life character's entrance is deliberately delayed.** With the Death-faction companion now rounding the group out to a five-person party (see characters.md — Relationships), let this group first settle into a working rhythm — including the new multi-pair group-combat dynamic (see worldbuilding.md — Partnered Casting: Group Combat) — before a Life-affiliated character enters the story. The point of waiting is for that eventual arrival to visibly shake up an already-established dynamic, rather than join a group still finding its footing. Who that Life character is and how they enter remains undecided — not yet chosen between the brainstormed options (regime defector, exchange student, rescue debt, etc.).
